## 4. Why the LIVE-FLOW Under Failed

### A. New York Shot Regression Was Underestimated

The model expected only slight New York improvement. Actual second-half New York shooting surged to:

- 22/36 FG, 61.1%
- 32 paint points on 16/23 shooting
- 55 second-half points

The primary miss was not Jonquel Jones regression. Jones remained inefficient and scored only four second-half points. The improvement came from:

- Breanna Stewart: 15 second-half points
- Pauline Astier: 12 second-half points
- Marine Fauthoux: 9 second-half points
- New York's broader rim and paint conversion

The model focused too narrowly on individual positive-regression candidates and not enough on team-level rim-access potential.

### B. A'ja Wilson's Positive Regression Was Correct but Too Large for the Under Thesis

Wilson had 10 points on 2/8 shooting at halftime. She then produced:

- 23 second-half points
- 9/14 FG
- 4/5 FT

The model recognized positive regression, but the Under recommendation treated that recovery as a counterweight rather than a possible major scoring engine. Wilson alone added 13 points above her first-half output.

### C. Efficiency Did Not Regress Enough at the Team Level

The first half showed apparent inflation:

- LVA 50% from three
- Combined FT shooting 26/28
- Young 7/9 FG
- Allen 4/5 FG

Some regression did occur:

- Jackie Young went 0/2 from the field after halftime.
- Rebecca Allen scored only three second-half points.
- Las Vegas fourth-quarter FG% fell to 37.5%.

But those regressions were offset by:

- New York 61.1% second-half FG
- Wilson 9/14 FG in the second half
- Chelsea Gray 4/5 FG and 2/3 from three after halftime
- Las Vegas 13/14 second-half FT

The model treated visible first-half outlier shooting as the dominant future signal. The actual game demonstrated cross-player replacement of efficiency: one hot scorer cooled, but other high-usage players heated up.

### D. Foul and Endgame Environment Added Scoring

The second half featured 23 made free throws on 24 attempts, including:

- Las Vegas 13/14
- New York 7/10
- Las Vegas 12/12 in the fourth quarter
- Jackie Young seven second-half points, all at the line

Late intentional fouling and technical/free-throw events pushed the total further above the model. The technical free throw at the start of the fourth and the late foul sequence materially increased scoring.

However, the Under was already in trouble before the final seconds because the third quarter produced 53 points and both teams maintained efficient half-court offense.

### E. Competitive Game-State Risk Was Underpriced

The halftime lead was eight, but New York reduced the margin to one multiple times in the second half. This created:

- Full starter minutes
- Aggressive offensive possessions
- No clock-kill phase
- Late free-throw extension

The model acknowledged competitiveness but still priced the second half around 90. The actual close-game environment warranted a wider upper tail.

## 6. What Requires Modification

### LIVE-FLOW Total Model

1. Add a competitive-game upper-tail adjustment when the halftime margin is 4–10 and both teams retain healthy primary creators.
2. Separate player-level shooting regression from team-level scoring regression. Hot-player cooling does not guarantee team scoring decline.
3. Add a rim-access recovery factor when a team has poor first-half FG% but continues generating paint attempts.
4. Increase the influence of first-half free-throw attempt rate and foul pressure, not just free-throw percentage.
5. Add an endgame foul-extension distribution for projected finals within two possessions.
6. Require at least 4.0 points of model-to-market value for a live total strike; this position had only 2.5 points against the book and should have remained a lean/pass.
7. Widen second-half total uncertainty bands when both teams have multiple elite creators capable of replacing each other's efficiency.

### Player-Prop Model

1. Replace fixed FTA assumptions with a broader, matchup-sensitive distribution.
2. Track drive frequency, opponent foul rate, and likely late-game foul role.
3. Preserve recency weighting but avoid interpreting a hit as proof that the mean was fully calibrated.
4. Record scoring-path attribution: field goals, threes, free throws, and minutes separately.
